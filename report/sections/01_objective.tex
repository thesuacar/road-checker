\section{Business / Objective Understanding}
\textbf{Research objective.}
The classification target was defined as \textit{Smooth} $=0$ and
\textit{Bumpy} $=1$, with Bumpy treated as the positive class.

From the intended application perspective, a false positive---a Smooth road incorrectly classified as Bumpy---is 
considered more costly than a false negative, since repeated false alarms could unnecessarily direct
inspection or maintenance resources towards roads that do not require intervention.
In the intended screening workflow, a Bumpy prediction would trigger further
inspection or prioritisation rather than immediate maintenance; consequently,
excessive false alarms would directly consume limited inspection resources.

However, precision alone is not an adequate optimisation criterion. A highly
conservative classifier could obtain very high precision simply by predicting Bumpy only
in a small number of extreme cases, while missing most genuinely bumpy roads. Therefore,
the primary model-selection metric was chosen as the $F_{0.5}$ score:

\begin{equation}
F_{0.5}
=
\frac{1.25\,(\mathrm{Precision})(\mathrm{Recall})}
     {0.25\,\mathrm{Precision}+\mathrm{Recall}}.
\end{equation}

The value $\beta=0.5$ weights precision more strongly than recall while still penalising models that achieve high precision by detecting too few Bumpy segments. Precision, recall, specificity, balanced accuracy, ROC--AUC and average precision were retained as supporting diagnostic metrics.
